# Animal (= Metazoa) Diversity

## Bilateria
Animals with bilateral symmetry.

**Synapomorphies:**
- Bilateral symmetry
- **Cephalization**
- **Triploblastic** tissues (3 layers)

**Cephalization:** formation of a head region, with centers of nervous control located at one end.
- Allows for free-living, motile animals.
- Indicates the direction of movement: the head contacts the environment first.
- Special senses are developed, and new sensory information is processed by the brain.

**Triploblastic tissues (3 germ layers):**
- **Ecto**derm: outermost layer, later lining the body surface
- **Meso**derm: middle layer
- **Endo**derm: innermost layer

## Protostomia vs. Deuterostomia
Bilaterians can be divided by how the mouth develops. Both are monophyletic groups.

| | **Protostomia** ("first mouth") | **Deuterostomia** ("second mouth") |
|---|---|---|
| Blastopore (first opening in the embryo) becomes | Mouth | Anus |
| Nerve cord | Ventral | Dorsal (in chordates) |
| Notes | Greatest radiation of invertebrates (most animal species) | Includes echinoderms and chordates, including vertebrates (you are a deuterostome) |

# Platyhelminthes (Flatworms)

- ~25,000-30,000 species.
- Free-living or symbiotic.

## Four Major Groups
- **Turbellaria:** free-living flatworms
- **Trematoda:** endoparasitic flukes (live *in* animals)
- **Monogenea:** ectoparasitic flukes (live *on* animals)
- **Cestoda:** endoparasitic tapeworms (live *in* animals)

## Symbiosis
- A close, long-term interaction between two different species.
- Types:
	- **Mutualism:** both species benefit.
	- **Commensalism:** one organism benefits while the other is neither helped nor harmed.
	- **Parasitism:** one organism benefits at the expense of the other.

# Lecture 10: Worms

## Useful Terms
- **Complete gut:** a digestive tract with a mouth and an anus (one-way flow).
- **Incomplete gut:** a digestive tract with a single opening (e.g., flatworms, cnidarians).
- **Monoecious:** one individual has both male and female reproductive organs (hermaphrodite).
- **Dioecious:** separate male and female individuals.
- **Hydrostatic skeleton:** a fluid-filled skeleton.

## Mollusca: Cephalopoda
- **Radula:** a rasping, tongue-like organ used in feeding (found in most molluscs).
- Cephalopoda includes squids, nautiluses, octopuses, and cuttlefish.
- *Cephalopod* means "head-foot": the foot is modified into a funnel (siphon) that expels water from the mantle cavity, plus arms/tentacles around the mouth.
- Carnivores: they capture food with their tentacles and bite it with a beak.
- Very complex eyes and a sophisticated brain.
- Most use ink as a smokescreen (nautiluses do not).
- Experts at camouflage.

# Annelida

- Protostome bilaterian animals (segmented worms).
- Earliest fossils are ~520 million years old.
- ~15,000+ living species: marine, freshwater, and terrestrial.
- **Synapomorphies:**
	- **Metameric segmentation**
	- **Setae** (bristles)
- Other features:
	- Hydrostatic skeleton
	- Complete gut (mouth and anus)

## Metameric Segmentation
- **Metamerism:** the body is composed of serial (repeated) parts.
- Segments are separated by **septa**.
- Allows more precise control of movement.

## Setae
- Bristles made of **chitin** (a tough polysaccharide).
- Used for anchoring and movement.
- In marine species, setae grow from **parapodia** (fleshy body projections). Earthworms and leeches have no parapodia.

## Polychaetes
- A paraphyletic group of mostly marine annelids.
- Setae grow from parapodia.
- Well-differentiated head with sense organs.
- Some are sedentary to sessile **suspension feeders**.
	- Tube worms live in calcareous (calcium carbonate) tubes and collect food with **tentacles**.
- Some are motile predators with jaws.
	- E.g., the Bobbit worm (*Eunice aphroditois*).

## Lumbricidae (Earthworms)
- Mostly terrestrial, with some freshwater species.
- Have setae but **no parapodia**.
- Monoecious.
- **Clitellum:** a secretory organ that forms the cocoon for eggs.
- Ecologically important: they enrich, mix, and aerate the soil.
- Can ingest roughly their own weight in soil every 24 hours, and move 10-18 tons of soil per acre annually.

## Hirudinida (Leeches)
- Mostly freshwater; some are marine or terrestrial.
- Monoecious, but reproduce by cross-fertilization (they exchange sperm with a partner).
- Setae are absent.
- Clitellum is present but only obvious during breeding.
- Some are temporary or permanent ectoparasites (many others are predators or scavengers).
	- Anterior and posterior suckers for attachment.
	- The gut can store large amounts of blood.

# Ecdysozoa

- Protostomes that **molt** their cuticle.
- **Major synapomorphy:** **ecdysis**, the molting of the cuticle (a non-cellular outer covering).
- Includes Nematoda, Onychophora, Tardigrada, and Arthropoda.

## Nematoda ("Thread"; Roundworms)
- Very diverse and abundant.
- Cuticle is non-chitinous and made of collagen (other ecdysozoans have a chitinous cuticle).
- Free-living, mutualistic, and parasitic species in a variety of habitats.
- No legs.
- Longitudinal muscles only.
- Dioecious.

## Onychophora ("Claw Bearer"; Velvet Worms)
- Numerous pairs of unjointed, clawed appendages.
- Chitin in the cuticle.
- Exclusively terrestrial.
- Most are predatory: they immobilize prey with slime ejected from slime glands through oral **papillae** near the head.

## Tardigrada (Water Bears)
- Four pairs of unjointed legs with claws.
- Chitinous cuticle that is shed.
- Microscopic; live in water films on moss and lichen, and in other damp habitats.
- Nervous system with a brain and ventral nerve cord.
- Circulatory and respiratory systems are absent.
- Mostly dioecious (some reproduce asexually). Females lay eggs inside the shed cuticle during ecdysis.
- Tubular mouth with a pair of **stylets** that pierce plant cells or small animal prey so the animal can suck out the contents.

**Cryptobiosis:**
- A state of suspended animation.
- The animal nearly dries out completely and its metabolism stops.
- Tardigrades in this state can withstand temperatures near absolute zero (absolute zero = -273.15 °C).
- For scale:
	- Oxygen melts at -218 °C
	- Helium boils at -269 °C
	- Helium solidifies only under pressure, at about -272 °C

# Arthropoda

Ecdysozoan protostome bilaterian animals; includes chelicerates (arachnids and relatives), crustaceans, myriapods, and insects.

**Synapomorphies:**
- Chitinous exoskeleton with articulated (jointed) appendages, shed through ecdysis.
- **Compound eyes** made of multiple ocular units (**ommatidia**) giving a wide visual area.
	- Each ommatidium is hexagonal with ~6-8 light-sensitive cells.
- Segmented bodies organized into **tagmata** (functionally specialized body regions).

## Chelicerata ("Claw Horn")
- **Synapomorphy:** the first pair of appendages are **chelicerae** (fang-like feeding appendages).
- Other features:
	- Antennae absent
	- 2 tagmata

**Three major clades:**
- **Pycnogonida:** sea spiders
- **Merostomata:** horseshoe crabs
- **Arachnida:** spiders, scorpions, harvestmen, ticks, and mites

### Merostomata ("Thigh Mouth")
- Horseshoe crabs; not true crabs (they are more closely related to arachnids).
- Shallow marine; found along the Atlantic coast of North America and in Southeast Asia.
- Carapace (shell) covers the head region.
- Only 4 living species.
- Book gills.
- **Telson** ("tail").

### Arachnida ("Spider")
- Spiders, scorpions, ticks, mites, and harvestmen.
- Over 100,000 described species; spiders (~50,000) and mites (~55,000) are the two largest groups.
- 2 tagmata.
- Breathe with book lungs and/or tracheae.
- Some (spiders) spin silk.

# Arthropods II: Myriapods

## Mandibulata
Myriapods, crustaceans, and hexapods have **mandibles** (mouthparts for biting, slicing, and chewing).

## Myriapoda
- Respire with **tracheae** (air tubes) that open through **spiracles**.
- 2 tagmata: head and trunk, with many **uniramous** appendages (one branch per leg; also found in chelicerates and hexapods).
- One pair of antennae.
- Compound eyes are *lost*; they have clusters of simple eyes instead.

**Two major clades:**
- **Chilopoda:** centipedes
- **Diplopoda:** millipedes

## Chilopoda ("Lip Foot")
- Centipedes.
- **One pair** of legs per trunk segment.
	- The number of pairs varies by species, from 15 up to over 170 (always an odd number).
	- The first pair is modified into venom fangs (forcipules).
- Active predators.
	- Secretive; hide under rocks and logs.
	- Dorsoventrally flattened (flat from top to bottom).
	- Immobilize prey with venom from the fangs.

## Diplopoda ("Double Foot")
- Millipedes.
- **Two pairs** of legs per trunk segment.
	- The first few segments have only 1 pair.
	- The remaining segments are fused **diplosegments**, each with 2 pairs.
- Slow-moving herbivores/detritivores with robust mouthparts for eating plant material.
- Because they are slow, they rely on defense:
	- Heavily armored cuticle
	- Noxious fluids exuded from the sides of the body

# Arthropods II: Crustacean Biology

- The name comes from the "crust" (hard shell) of the group.
- Includes crabs, lobsters, shrimp, and barnacles.
- ~67,000 species.
- Aquatic, mostly marine; most respire with gills.
- Free-living or sessile.
- Predatory, suspension feeding, or parasitic.

**Synapomorphies and features:**
- **Two pairs of antennae**
- **Biramous** appendages (two branches per jointed appendage)
- Compound eyes, often on stalks
- Body usually divided into 2-3 tagmata (e.g., cephalothorax and abdomen)

# Hexapoda

- Includes the insects.
- **Synapomorphies:**
	- Three pairs of legs on the thorax
	- Three tagmata: head, thorax, abdomen

## Insect Success
- 1 million+ described species.
- Ecological diversity.
- Segmentation: 3 tagmata, with 3 segments in the thorax.
- Many are small, so they can exploit tiny habitats.
- Watertight (waxy) exoskeleton.
- Flight.
- Specialized mouthparts.
- Coevolution with plants (e.g., pollination).
- Metamorphosis.

### Plants Get Their Revenge
- Some plants have seeds that mimic feces, which ensures dispersal by dung beetles.

## Metamorphosis
- **Complete metamorphosis:** egg → larva → pupa → adult (e.g., flies, butterflies, beetles, ants, bees).
- **Incomplete metamorphosis:** egg → nymph → adult, with no pupal stage (e.g., grasshoppers, true bugs).

## The 4 Most Important Insect Clades
- **Coleoptera:** beetles
- **Hymenoptera:** bees, wasps, and ants
- **Diptera:** flies
- **Lepidoptera:** butterflies and moths

# Echinodermata ("Spiny Skin")

- Deuterostomes; includes sea stars, sea urchins, sea cucumbers, brittle stars, and sea lilies.
- ~7,000 living species.
- Exclusively marine.
- Economically important (we eat some, e.g., sea urchin roe and sea cucumber).
- Ecologically important.

**Synapomorphies:**
- Pentaradial symmetry
- Water vascular system (locomotion and sometimes feeding)
- Calcareous endoskeleton

## Pentaradial Symmetry (5-fold)
- Adults have no head and show 5-fold symmetry around a central disc.
- Larvae have bilateral symmetry.

## Water Vascular System
- A hydrostatic pressure system that operates the **tube feet**.
- Used for locomotion and sometimes feeding.
- Tube feet extend from the **ambulacral grooves**.

## Calcareous Endoskeleton
- A **test** made of small calcareous plates (**ossicles**) bound together with collagen.
- Covered by epidermis (skin) with spines and **pedicellariae**.
	- Pedicellariae are pincer-like structures that remove debris from around the respiratory structures and aid in defense.

## Reproduction
- Sexual, or asexual through **fragmentation**.
- **Fragmentation:** breaking the body into pieces, some or all of which develop into adults. It must be accompanied by **regeneration** (regrowth of lost body parts).
