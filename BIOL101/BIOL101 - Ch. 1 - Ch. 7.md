## Zoology
- A subdivision of biology (the study of life).
- The scientific study of animals.

### What Is an Animal?
- Animals are **multicellular**.
	- They are composed of many eukaryotic cells (cells with a true nucleus) that show a "division of labor."
- Animals are **heterotrophic**: they ingest (eat) raw materials from the environment to live.
	- *hetero* = different
	- *troph* = nourishment / food

#### Comparing Eukaryotes

| Group | Cells | Nutrition | Animal? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amoebas** | Unicellular | Heterotrophic (they eat) | No: only one cell |
| **Plants** | Multicellular | Autotrophic: make their own food by photosynthesis | No |
| **Fungi** | Mostly multicellular (yeasts are unicellular) | Heterotrophic, but they *absorb* nutrients rather than eat | No |
| **Animals** | Multicellular | Heterotrophic: they *ingest* food | Yes |

**Only animals are multicellular heterotrophs that ingest their food.**

#### Examples of animals
- Sponge
- Sea fan
- Pyrosome (a colonial tunicate)

All three are multicellular heterotrophs that take in food: sponges and pyrosomes filter bacteria and plankton from the water, and sea fans catch plankton with their tentacles.

## What Is Science?

- Science is dynamic and changing.
- ***"Modern science seeks to explain natural phenomena in natural terms."*** (National Center for Science Education)
	- Science prefers natural explanations over supernatural ones because of a voluntary, self-imposed methodology (**methodological naturalism**), not because it claims to disprove the supernatural.

## How Is Science Accomplished? (*Lecture II, 8/27/26*)

**The common currency of science is observation and hypothesis.**
- Progress happens when all scientists use the same process to discover new information.
- Science works by building hypotheses to explain observations.
- Hypotheses are tested against further observations and experiments.
- We choose the best explanation based on the evidence **currently available**, so science is open to change.

### Fact, Hypothesis, Theory, Law
- **Fact:** an observation that has been repeatedly confirmed.
- **Hypothesis:** a tentative, testable explanation of a natural phenomenon that can be supported or refuted.
- **Theory:** a well-tested, well-substantiated explanation of a natural phenomenon, based on a large body of observations and experiments.
- **Law:** a concise (often mathematical) description of a consistent pattern in nature. A law describes *what* happens; a theory explains *why*.

When science is defined and used properly, we can use facts and hypotheses to build theories about ourselves and the world around us.

### The Scientific Method (Hypothetico-Deductive Approach)
1. Observation
2. Question
3. Hypothesis (from which testable predictions are deduced)
4. Empirical test
5. Conclusion

### Theory vs. "Theory"
The word "theory" is used differently in everyday language and in science.

| Everyday use | Scientific use |
|---|---|
| Conjecture | Well supported |
| Speculation | Testable ideas |
| Opinion | Based on objective data |

**Fact of gravity vs. theory of gravity**
- Fact: objects fall toward the center of the Earth.
- Law: Newton's law of universal gravitation describes the force mathematically.
- Theory: explains *why* gravity works (e.g., Einstein's general relativity).

### What Science Is NOT
- The solution to all problems and questions
- Free of rules (it follows a systematic methodology)
- A road to absolute "truth"
- Out to *prove* anything (it supports or refutes hypotheses)
- 100% certain
- The final answer
- Infallible (not perfect)
- Unbiased (scientists are human; the method is designed to reduce bias)
- Always used properly

## What Is Evolution?

- **Descent with modification** (Darwin, 1859).
	- **Descent:** passing from generation to generation (descended from ancestors).
	- **Modification:** change (you look a little different from your parents).
- **Modern definition:** a change in the frequency of **alleles** (different forms of a gene) within a population from one generation to the next.
	- A **population** (a group of individuals of the same species at a given time) is the unit of evolution.

**So what's the problem?** *"Evolution is only a theory..."*
- Evolution is both a fact and a theory: we can observe it happening (fact), and the theory of evolution explains how it works.

# History of Evolutionary Thought (*Lecture III, Tuesday, 9/1/26*)

- Early ideas of transmutation of species came from Greece, China, and Persia.
- Medieval Europe: ***Scala Naturae*** (the "ladder of nature"), a fixed hierarchy of life.
- 16th-17th century: **Scientific Revolution**
	- Rejection of classical ideas and a more mechanistic view of nature.
- **James Burnett, Lord Monboddo:** argued that humans were related to apes.
- **Erasmus Darwin:** Charles's grandfather; hypothesized common ancestry of organisms.
- **Uniformitarianism** (Hutton, Lyell): the laws of chemistry and physics have remained constant throughout Earth's history.
	- Natural processes of the past are occurring today ("the present is the key to the past").

### Jean-Baptiste Lamarck
- French soldier (war hero) and naturalist.
- *Philosophie Zoologique* (1809)
	- **Inheritance of acquired characteristics** (now discredited as a mechanism)
	- Transmutation (physical change) of species
	- Evolution is gradual, subtle, and imperceptible
	- Proposed that species are related by descent (though he believed simple life arose repeatedly by spontaneous generation)

### Darwin's Postulates
Evolution by natural selection is the logical outcome of the following:
1. Individuals within populations are variable.
2. Variations among individuals are heritable.
3. In every generation, some individuals are more successful at surviving and reproducing than others (differences in **fitness**).
4. Survival and reproduction are nonrandom.

# Mechanisms of Evolution

Evolution is a change in allele frequency within a population from one generation to the next. Mechanisms that change allele frequencies:
- **Mutation:** an abrupt, heritable change in a gene (the ultimate source of new alleles).
- **Migration (gene flow):** genes move from one population to another.
- **Genetic drift:** random sampling error over generations (strongest in small populations).
- **Natural selection:** alleles change in frequency based on their effect on fitness (the only mechanism that consistently produces adaptation).

Natural selection and genetic drift are the main drivers of allele-frequency change.

Evolution is the underlying principle that guides zoology. So how do we study and recognize animal diversity?

### Scale
- **Microevolution:** evolution within a single population (small scale).
- **Macroevolution:** evolution above the species level, resulting from speciation (large scale).

## Speciation
- **Speciation:** the process by which new species arise.
	- **Species** (biological species concept): a group of interbreeding individuals of common descent that are reproductively isolated from other groups.
- Two general modes:
	- **Allopatric:** populations are physically isolated.
	- **Sympatric:** a continuous population with no geographic isolation.

### Allopatric Speciation
- Occurs when populations become physically isolated, leading to reproductive isolation. Isolation happens by either:
	- **Dispersal:** individuals migrate from one area to another.
	- **Vicariance:** a population is divided by a physical barrier (mountain, river, valley, etc.).

# Organizing Animal Diversity

- **Taxonomy:** the ordering and naming of organisms.
	- *Why bother?* An estimated ~70-90% of species are still unrecognized (undescribed).
- **Systematics:** the study of evolutionary relationships and classification of organisms.
	- **Phylogenetic systematics (cladistics):** classification based on common ancestry.

# Animal Ancestors and Sponges

## Phylogenetic Systematics
- **Cladogram:** a branching diagram of ancestor-descendant relationships.
	- **Clade:** a natural (monophyletic) group made up of an ancestor and all of its descendants.
- **Sister taxa:** the two descendant groups of a common ancestor.
- **Synapomorphy:** a shared, derived characteristic of two or more taxa; used to diagnose clades.

**Prokaryotic cells** (for comparison with eukaryotes):
- Reproduce by binary fission (asexual)
- No true nucleus
- Lack membrane-bound organelles
- Mostly single-celled organisms

# Porifera

- Includes the **sponges**; the name means "pore-bearing."
- Among the most basal animals (generally considered the sister taxon to all other animals).

## Synapomorphies
- **Aquiferous system** ("water system"): a system of canals that pumps water for feeding, respiration, and excretion.
- **Spicules:** skeletal elements.
- **Sessile** as adults (vs. motile): they are attached and do not move.
- No mouth or gut.

## Anatomy
- The skeleton is made of **spicules** (microscopic spines) and/or **spongin** (a collagen protein).
- Spicules are made of calcium carbonate or silica. Glass sponges have silica spicules, which are literally glass!

## Aquiferous System: Pores and Canals
- **Ostia** (sing. *ostium*): incurrent openings (water in).
- **Osculum** (pl. *oscula*): excurrent opening (water out).
- The complexity of the system varies among sponges.
- **Choanocytes:** collar cells with flagella that circulate water and trap food.
- **Mesohyl:** the gel-like middle layer containing cells and spicules.

# Cnidaria and Body Symmetry

- Cnidarians and ctenophores have **radial symmetry**: body parts are arranged around an oral-aboral axis.
- Cnidarians have two tissue layers (**diploblastic**):
	- **Epidermis:** outermost layer, lining the body.
	- **Mesoglea:** the middle, jelly-like layer connecting the other two.
	- **Gastrodermis:** innermost layer, lining the gut.
- Major clades: **Anthozoa** and **Medusozoa**.

## Synapomorphies of Cnidaria
- **Cnidocytes:** specialized stinging cells.
- Free-swimming larva (**planula**).
- Radial symmetry, with the mouth surrounded by tentacles.
- Polymorphism (different body forms) in some species: **polyp** and **medusa**.
