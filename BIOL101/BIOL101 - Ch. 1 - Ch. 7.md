### What is an animal?
- Animals are multicellular
	- This means they are composed of multiple eukaryotic cells; or the "division of labor"
- Animals are heterotrophic (*ingest or eat raw materials from the environment in order to live*)
	- *hetero* meaning different
	- *trophic* meaning energy


#### Eukaryotes with true nucleus!

**Amoebas!** They are heterotrophic and eat but it is unicellular. This does not classify as an animal as it's only one cell.

**Plants!** Plants are multicellular however they are autotrophic, meaning they create their own food using photosynthesis. 

**Fungi!** Fungi are multicellular and heterotrophic but they don't eat but absorb!

Only animals are multicellular heterotrophs that eat.

- Sponge

- Pyrosome

This is due to the fact that they are all multicellular as well as "eat" by digesting bacteria for energy. They are heterotroph and multicellular.

## How is Science Accomplished? (*Lecture II 8/27/26*)


**Common Currency of Science is Observation and Hypothesis**
- Advancement is only when all scientists use the same process to discover new information
- Science works by building hypotheses to explain observations
- Testing hypotheses and observations.
- Choosing among these hypotheses to select best explanation based on evidence that is ***currently available today.***
	- *This means that science is open to change.* 

**Fact:** Science that has been repeatedly confirmed

**Hypothesis:** tentative statement about a natural phenomenon that can be tested, supported or refuted.

**Theory:** highly probable, well-tested and substantiated of a natural phenomenon based on a large collection of observations and experiments.

**When science is defined and used properly, we can use facts and hypotheses to formulate theories about our lives and the world around us.**

## What is Evolution?

- Descent with modification (Darwin, 1859)

**Descent**: From generation to generation. (Descended from ancestors)
**Modification:** Change (you look a little different from parents)

**Modern definition:** Change in frequency of alleles (a different form of a gene) within a population from one generation to the next pop. (group of individuals of the same species at a given time) is the unit of evolution

**So what's the problem?**
*"Evolution is only a theory..."*

It can be fact and theory as we can observe it happening.

# Mechanisms of Evolution

- Evolution is a change in allele frequency within a population from one generation to the next
- Mechanisms for a change in allele frequency?
	- Mutation - abrupt change to a gene
	- Migration - genes move from one population to another 
	- Genetic Drift - random sampling error over generations (Most Common)
	- Natural Selection - considering genes as they relate to fitness. (Most Common)


- Evolution is the underlying principle that guides zoology. How do we study and recognize animal diversity?


- Microevolution - evolution within a single population or small scale
- Macroevolution - evolution above the species level; caused by speciation (large scale)

## Speciation

- Speciation
	- The process by which new species arise (species: group of interbreeding individuals of common descent that are reproductively isolated from other groups)
- 2 general modes of speciation
	- Allopatric
		- Occurs in small populations that are physically isolated
	- Sympatric
		- Continuous population (no geographic isolation)

### Allopatric Speciation

- Allopatric
	- Occurs in small populations that become physically isolated leading to reproductive isolation (either dispersal or vicariance)
- Dispersal 
	- Individuals migrate from one area to another
- Vicariance
	- Population geographically divided by a physical barrier (mountain, river, valley , etc..)

# Porifera

- Includes **sponges**, name refers to the porus body

## Synapomorphies:
