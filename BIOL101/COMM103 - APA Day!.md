
## Why do we use sources:

- **To show evidence**
	- Our thoughts do not come from a vacuum
- **To join conversation:**
	- By using sources, we join larger conversation
- **We are not the experts:**
	- The expertise of others informs our positions

## Why do we cite sources?
- **Give credit**:
	- Respect other ideas and work
	- Passing someone else's idea off as your own is plagiarism!
- **Build credibility**:
	- Show your audience that you are trustworthy
	- Prove you got your evidence from a legitimate, reliable source.

## Plagiarism is:

- Stealing someone else's words and ideas and claiming them as your own.
	- Can be a deliberate copy and paste or simply failing to cite where your info is from
- What also counts as plagiarism
	- Self plagiarism aka double dipping, using previous work for current classes
	- Not citing secondary sources

## Building Citations

**In-text citation:**
- (Sub Point #2 (c.n)) To complement the summer weather during Christmas, Australians like to have outdoor barbecues (Martin, 2014)
**Ref Page Citations:**
- Martin, A (2014, Aug-Sep) blah blah blah blah

## Direct Quote vs Paraphrasing:

- Direct quote:
	- When you use information from an outside source and copy what they say word for word
- **Paraphrasing**: Using information from an outside source but not copying their writing verbatim


## Paraphrasing In-Text:
- Beginning of sentence:
	- According to Marcus (2026), the seahawks are the superior football team in the NFC west.
	- Lee et al. (2025) states that drinking matcha daily is a healthier option than coffee
- **End of Sentence**:
	- Halloween is the best holiday (Homan, 2021)
	- Students present better in class rather than in office hours (Ramirez, 2019)

## Direct Quotes:
- **Author's Last Name, Year, Page/Paragraph #**
	- Marcus (2025) stated, "Blah blah" (p. 9).
	- "Autumn is the best time of year" (Lee & Bianchi, 2026, Para. 6)
- **Use a page number first** -- If there is no page number, use paragraph
- Remember, the only difference here is the page/paragraph #.

## Called REFERENCE PAGE not WORKS CITED
- Bolded and centered
- All refs should be listed in alphabetical order by author's last name
	- Second line of source should be indented, which is called a hanging indent
