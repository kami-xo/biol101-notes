

# Animal (=Metazoa Diversity)

- **Bilateria**: Animals with bilateral symmetry
- **Synapomorphies**:
	- Bilateral symmetry
	- **Cephalization**:
		- Centers of the nervous control located at one end: the head!
	- Triploblastic tissues:
		- 3 layers "tri"


**Cephalization**: Formation of a head region
	Allows for free living, motile animals
	Indicates direction of movement - head contacts environment first
	Special senses are developed. New sensory information processed by brain

**Triploblastic Tissues (3 Layers):**
- Ecto-derm - Outermost tissue later lining body
- Meso-derm - Middle layer 
- Endo-derm - Innermost tissue layer

## Protostomia vs Deuterostomia

- Bilaterians can be divided by development of the mouth

**Proto-stomia** "First Mouth":
- Monophyletic group of animals
- Became the greatest radiation of invertebrate species (most species)
**Deu-terostomia** "Second Mouth":
- Monophyletic group of animals
- Lineage dating to the vertebrates (You are a deuterostome)

**Major Differences in development:**
- Protostome: First opening to develop (**blastopore**), becomes mouth, nerve cord, ventral
- Deuterostome: Blastopore becomes anus; nerve cord dorsal.

# Platyhelminthes

## Four Major Groups:

- Turbellaria - Free-living flatworms
- Trematoda - Endoparasitic flukes (live in animals)
- Monogenea - Ectoparasitic flukes (live on animals)
- Cestoda - Endoparasitic tapeworms (live in animals)

## Symbiosis
- Interaction of two different species 
- One or both get benefits
- Types of symbiosis:
	  - **Mutualism**: Both species benefit
	  - **Commensalism:** One organism benefits while the other is neither helped nor harmed
	  - **Parasitism:** One organism benefits at the expense of the other

## Cephalopoda
- Squids, nautiluses, octopi, and cuttlefish
- "Head Fish"
	- modified as a funnel to expel water from mantle cavity
	- Tentacles to capture food around beak (carnivores)
- They also have very complex eye and a very sophisticated brain
- Use ink as a smoke screen
- Experts at camouflage

**Annelida**: Protostome bilaterian animals
- Earliest fossils are 520 million years old
- 15k living species, marine, freshwater terrestrial
- **Synapomorphies**:
	- Metameric segmentation
	- Setae (bristles for movement)
- Other features:
	- **Hydrostatic:** fluid-filled skeleton
	- Complete gut (mouth and anus)
## Metameric Segmentation
- Metamerism - Composed of serial parts
- Segments separated by **septa**
- Allows for more precise control of movement

## Setae
- Bristles made of **chitin** (hard carbs)
- Used for stabilization and movement
- In marine species, grow from parapodia (not in earthworms or leeches)


## Polychaetes
- Paraphyletic group of mostly marine annelids
- Setae grow from parapodia
- Well differentiated head with sense organs
- Some are sedentary to sessile **suspension feeders**
- Tube worms, live in calcareous tubes
- Food collection by **tentacles**

- Some are motile predators with jaws
- Eg. Bobbit worm (*Eunice aphroditois*)

incomplete gut:
 a primitive animal digestive system with a single opening or to a human medical symptom where you feel you have not fully emptied your bowels.

## Lumbricidae
- Includes earthworms
- Freshwater and terrestrial species
- Have setae but *no parapodia*
- Monoecious
- **Clitellum** - secretory organ
- Ecologically important - enrich, mix, and aerate the soil
- Ingest their own weight in soil every 24 hours, pass 10-18 tons of soil annually per acre.

## Hirudinida (leeches)
- Most terrestrial
- Monoecious, but utilize in cross-fertilization
	- meaning have both female and male reproductive organs
- Setae absent
- Clitellum, but internal
- Some are either temporary or permanent ectoparasites
	- Anterior and posterior suckers for attachment.
	- Gut can store large amounts of blood

## Nematoda "Thread"
- Diverse
- Non-chitinous exoskeleton composed of collagen (other ecdysozoans have a chitinous cuticle)
- Free living, mutualistic, and parasitic species
- Live in a variety of habitats
- No legs ("Round worms")
- Longitudinal muscles only.

Dioecious means an organism has separate male and female individual plants or animals rather than having both sexes on the same body

## Ecdysozoa
- Major synapomorphy: ecdysis - molting of cuticle (non-cellular level)

## Onychophora ("Claw Bearer")
- Velvet Worms
- Numerous pairs of unjointed appendages w/ claws
- Chitin in exoskeletons
- Exclusively terrestrial
- Most are predatory - immobilize prey with slime ejected from slime glands in anterior **papillae**

## Tardigrada

- Water bears
- Four pairs of unjointed legs with claws
- Most have chitinous exoskeletons that are shed
- Microscopic, live in water droplets and moss and lichen

- Complex nervous system
- Circulatory and respiratory systems completely lacking
- Dioecious, females lay eggs in cuticle during ecdysis, fertilized either sexually and asexually.

- Tubular mouth contains a pair of **stylets**- pierce plant or animal prey and inject with digestive ....

**Cryptobiosis**:
- suspended animation
- nearly dry out completely
- metabolism stops
- Can withstand temps -273C
- oxygen melts at -218C
- helium boils @ -269C
- helium melts @ -272C
- Abs. Zero = -273C

**Arthropoda** - Ecdysozoan protostome bilaterian animals; includes arachnids, crustaceans, and insects

**Synapomorphies:**
- Chitinous exoskeleton with an articulated (jointed) appendages.
- **Compound** eyes (multiple ommatidia)
- Segmented bodies (tagmata functional statements)

- Exoskeleton composed of chitin (shed through ecdysis) with articulated (jointed appendages)
**Compound eyes**: composed of multiple ocular units (ommatidia); wide visual area
	**Ommatidia** are hexagonal with 6-8 light sensitive cells
## Chelicerata "Clawed"

- Synapomorphy - First pair of appendages developed as chelicerae (fang like jaws)
- Other features
	- Antennae lost
	- 2 tagmata

**Three Major Clades of Chelicerata**:
- Pycnogonida (Sea Spider)
- Merostomata (Horseshoe Crabs)
- Arachnida (Spiders, Scorpions, Harvestmen, Ticks, Mites)
